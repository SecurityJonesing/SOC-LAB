SOC Lab — Diagrams

Six reference diagrams from the build: topology, the bridge/socket lesson, the alert pipeline, agent-identity governance, the recovery ladder, and the phase map. Scroll down for all six.

1. Lab network topology

Every packet crosses pfSense on the way between VLANs. The switch only decides which VLAN a port belongs to — pfSense decides what's allowed to cross. The dashed green line is the one hole in the Range VLAN's fence: logs only, out to Wazuh on port 1514.

2. Why Kali went dark — the bridge is a wall socket

A VM plugs into a bridge, not a NIC. Move the bridge's uplink cable to a different NIC, and every VM still pointed at the old bridge goes dark — silently, with no error anywhere.

3. How an alert is born — two layers, one destination

A log is data. An alert is a log that matched a rule. Host-based telemetry (Sysmon) and network-based telemetry (Suricata off a SPAN port) both feed the Wazuh manager independently — correlating both is the high-value skill.

4. Phase D — the agent is an identity, not a script

The triage agent has its own credential, least-privilege scope, and its own audit trail — never your personal login. Its output is a suggested verdict, a draft, not a decision. You confirm or override, logged separately.

5. The recovery ladder — climb down until something works

Never make a change that could cut off your only access path. Confirm the next rung down works before you need it — the green rungs are physical and need no network, no config, no Java. That's why they're always the true fallback.

6. Phase map — what unlocks what

A.5 must pass before C — you don't run attack code on an unfenced network. B must pass before C — no SIEM, nothing to detect with. G is deferred, attempted only once F and E are stable, on its own dedicated VM.
